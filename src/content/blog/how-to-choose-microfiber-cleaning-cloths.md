If you only remember one sentence: **choose microfiber cleaning cloths by four specs — GSM weight, blend ratio, weave/construction, and edge finish — then match them to the job, not to the cheapest price.** A 300 GSM, 80/20, warp-knit, laser-sealed cloth covers about 80% of professional cleaning tasks. The other 20% is just a heavier GSM or a scrubbier blend away.

We make and export microfiber cloths in these specs every week, so this is a spec sheet, not a catalog pitch. Below is the decision framework buyers actually use when they are filling a container.

## Start here: match the spec to the job

| Use case | GSM | Blend | Weave | Edge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glass, optics, screens | 300–350 | 80/20 | Warp-knit, low pile | Laser / ultrasonic sealed |
| General wiping & dusting | 200–300 | 80/20 | Warp-knit | Stitched or sealed |
| Heavy-duty / kitchen | 300–350 | 70/30 | Warp-knit, scrub | Stitched |
| Buffing & polishing | 350+ | 80/20 | Woven, plush pile | Stitched |
| Healthcare / cleanroom | 300–350 | 80/20 | Warp-knit | Sealed + color-coded |

If you are not sure where you land, 300 GSM / 80/20 / warp-knit / sealed is the safe default and the single most-requested spec we produce.

## GSM — the number that decides everything

**GSM (grams per square meter)** is the cloth's weight, and it is the first line of any real quote. It is not "thicker = better" — it is "right weight for the surface."

- **200–300 GSM** — general purpose. Light enough to fold and detail, heavy enough to carry water. The workhorse for dusting, counters and everyday wiping.
- **300–350 GSM** — glass, automotive and heavy-duty. Denser weave = fewer streaks and more scrub without scratching.
- **350+ GSM** — buffing and polishing. Plush, high-pile construction that lifts residue off a finished surface without marring it.

A common mistake is over-specifying GSM for glass: a 350+ GSM plush cloth can leave fibers on a pane. For glass, density beats bulk.

## Blend: 80/20 vs 70/30

The blend is the polyester-to-polyamide ratio, and it changes the *feel* and the *job*:

- **80% polyester / 20% polyamide** — more polyamide means more absorbency and a softer hand. This is the default for glass, dusting and most general wiping.
- **70% polyester / 30% polyamide** — stiffer and scrubbier, better on textured or stuck-on messes. Slightly less absorbent but more aggressive.

Both are genuine microfiber. The ratio is a tuning knob, not a quality grade. If a supplier cannot tell you the blend, that is a red flag — see our [7-point manufacturer vetting checklist](/blog/how-to-vet-cleaning-products-manufacturer/).

## Weave and construction

- **Warp-knit** — the standard for cleaning cloths. Stable, low-lint, holds its shape through hundreds of washes. Most of what you will source is warp-knit.
- **Woven (terry or plush)** — plusher, used for buffing and drying where you want a thicker pile.
- **Nonwoven** — for wipes and disposables; different substrate family entirely. Our guide to [spunlace, spunbond, meltblown and needle-punched nonwovens](/blog/spunlace-vs-spunbond-vs-meltblown-vs-needle-punched/) explains when each belongs in a product.

For reusable cloths, warp-knit is the safe answer 9 times out of 10.

## Split pile — the detail that separates real from fake

"Split" is the keyword. During finishing, each microfiber filament is sliced open into hooked edges; those hooks are what mechanically trap dirt and pull liquid by capillary action. **Non-split** microfiber (often a bonded or printed imitation) looks almost identical on a shelf but cleans poorly and pills after a few washes.

Always write **"split-weave microfiber"** on the spec, and confirm it in a sample before you commit. This is also the point where [microfiber pulls ahead of cotton](/blog/microfiber-vs-cotton-cleaning-cloths/) on lint and wash-life.

## Edge finish — small detail, real lint difference

- **Laser-cut / ultrasonic-sealed** — cleanest edge, sheds the least lint. Spec this for glass, optics and healthcare where a stray fiber is a defect.
- **Stitched border** — cheaper, perfectly fine for general and heavy-duty wiping.
- **Glued edge** — avoid. The glue lets go after a handful of washes and leaves residue.

## Color coding and certifications

**Color coding** is non-negotiable for janitorial and healthcare buyers: assign red / blue / green / yellow to restroom, glass, kitchen and floor so one cloth never crosses zones. It is a cheap upgrade that buyers notice.

**Certifications** matter the moment the cloth touches skin or enters the EU/UK retail chain. Ask for a *currently valid* [OEKO-TEX® STANDARD 100](/blog/what-is-oeko-tex/) number — not a screenshot of someone else's. Recycled-PET (rPET) lines and [OEKO-TEX® MADE IN GREEN](/blog/what-is-oeko-tex/) are the defensible sustainability story if your customers ask.

## How to verify you got genuine microfiber

Four checks you can run on a sample before a container ships:

1. **Water-bead test** — drop water on the cloth; real split microfiber grabs and spreads it instead of beading.
2. **GSM check** — weigh a 40×40 cm cloth; a true 300 GSM cloth is about 48 g. Light means the spec was cut.
3. **Edge check** — sealed, not glued.
4. **Cert check** — valid OEKO-TEX® number on file.

## FAQ

**Q: What GSM microfiber cloth should I buy?**
A: 200–300 GSM for general wiping and dusting, 300–350 GSM for glass and heavy-duty, 350+ GSM for buffing and polishing. Heavier is not automatically better — glass and detailing want a denser, tighter weave, not just more grams.

**Q: What does 80/20 vs 70/30 microfiber mean?**
A: It is the polyester-to-polyamide ratio. 80% polyester / 20% polyamide is more absorbent and softer; 70/30 is stiffer and scrubbier for textured surfaces. Both are genuine split microfiber; the ratio just shifts the feel and job.

**Q: What is split vs non-split microfiber?**
A: Split microfiber has filaments sliced open into hooked edges during finishing — that is what traps dirt and liquid. Non-split (often printed or bonded) looks similar but cleans poorly and pills fast. Always confirm "split-weave" on the quote.

**Q: Which edge finish is best for microfiber cloths?**
A: Laser-cut or ultrasonic-sealed edges shed less lint than stitched borders, which matters for glass and healthcare. Stitched edges are cheaper and fine for general wiping. Avoid glued edges — they separate after a few washes.

**Q: How should I color-code microfiber cloths?**
A: Assign one color per zone — e.g. red restroom, blue glass, green kitchen, yellow floor — so a single cloth never crosses between contamination zones. Color coding is a selling point for janitorial and healthcare buyers.

**Q: How can I tell if a microfiber cloth is genuine?**
A: Four quick checks: (1) water-bead test — real split microfiber grabs water instead of beading it; (2) GSM — weigh a 40×40 cm cloth; a true 300 GSM cloth is ~48 g; (3) edge — sealed, not glued; (4) ask for a valid OEKO-TEX® STANDARD 100 number. The manufacturer vetting checklist covers the rest.
